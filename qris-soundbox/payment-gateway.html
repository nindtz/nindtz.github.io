Payment Gateway Owner-Role Integration

Let your merchants hear every QRIS payment at the counter. Your gateway takes the Owner role; the merchant's phone runs the QRIS Soundbox app as a branch and drives the soundbox.

Summary

We propose that the payment gateway (PG) takes the Owner role in the QRIS Soundbox system, so any PG merchant can pair a QRIS Soundbox phone as a Branch and get instant voice announcements of paid QRIS transactions.

In practice, the PG shows each merchant outlet a pairing QR code in its merchant dashboard or app. The merchant scans it with the QRIS Soundbox app. From then on, the PG pushes a small signed message for every successful payment at that outlet, and the app forwards it to the soundbox over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi.

The PG builds no hardware and no app. It provides a pairing QR per outlet and a payment-event push through the merchant's own Telegram bot. The app handles everything after that.

How QRIS Soundbox works today

QRIS Soundbox is a low-cost e-paper speaker for Indonesian SMEs that announces each QRIS payment out loud. It pairs with the QRIS Soundbox Android app (SoundboxBridge, Google Play package id.nindita.qrissbbridge), which feeds it payment events.

A merchant with several outlets runs two kinds of phone:

RoleWho runs itWhat it does
Owner phoneBusiness ownerDetects incoming QRIS payments and relays them to every branch. Holds the master QRIS and soundbox settings.
Branch phoneCashier at each outletPaired to the owner by scanning the owner's QR code or tapping an intent link. Receives relayed payments and drives the outlet's soundbox.

Relay runs over Telegram (TDLib in a foreground service) with FCM push as a wake-up. There is no QRIS Soundbox server: the product is sold buy-once.

The current model has two weaknesses that a PG can remove:

  • The owner phone must stay on, online and signed in, or every branch goes silent.
  • All branches hear all payments. Cashiers tell outlets apart by adding a small suffix to the amount (Rp1 for outlet A, Rp2 for outlet B).

Proposed model: PG as Owner

The PG replaces the owner phone. It already knows, server-side, the moment a QRIS payment settles and which outlet (store or terminal ID) it belongs to. So it can notify exactly the right branch, with no owner phone in the middle.

For merchants

  • Per-outlet announcements: each soundbox speaks only its own outlet's payments.
  • Nothing to keep running at head office.
  • Fewer "fake transfer screenshot" losses: confirmation comes from the PG itself.

For the PG

  • A soundbox offering at near-zero cost: no hardware stock, no app to build.
  • Stickier merchants and a visible presence at the counter.
  • A small, standard integration.

What does not change

  • Customers pay the PG's QRIS (static or dynamic) exactly as today.
  • Settlement, refunds and reporting stay with the PG. The soundbox only announces.

Integration flow

Pairing happens once per branch phone. After that, every settled payment flows PG → QRIS Soundbox app → soundbox, with no owner phone involved.

Technical specification (v1 draft)

1. Pairing QR code

The PG shows one pairing QR per outlet, inside its authenticated merchant dashboard or app. The QR encodes a URI that the app opens directly (scanned in-app, or tapped as an intent link on the same phone):

qrissoundbox://pair?d=<base64url(JSON)>

Decoded JSON:

{
  "v": 1,
  "role": "owner",
  "provider": "PGCODE",
  "merchant_name": "Kopi Senja",
  "outlet_id": "OUT-0012",
  "outlet_name": "Kopi Senja - Bekasi Timur",
  "qris": "00020101021126...6304ABCD",
  "pin": "1234",
  "transport": { "type": "telegram_bot", "bot": "KopiSenjaKasirBot" },
  "pairing_token": "pt_8f3c...",
  "expires_at": "2026-11-09T22:00:00+07:00"
}
FieldRequiredNotes
vyesSchema version, 1
roleyesAlways owner: tells the app to pair as a branch of this source
provideryesShort PG code, shown in the app
merchant_name, outlet_nameyesShown on the phone and the soundbox screen
outlet_idyesPG's store or terminal ID; every event carries it
qrisnoThe outlet's static QRIS string, so the soundbox can display it and generate amount-embedded QR
pinnoSoundbox settings PIN, same as an owner phone sends today
transportyesThe merchant's Telegram bot username (section 2)
pairing_tokenyesOne-time, short-lived (suggest 10 minutes); redeemed for device credentials
expires_atyesAfter this the app refuses the QR and asks for a fresh one

2. Event transport: the merchant's own Telegram bot

QRIS Soundbox runs no backend, so events always travel over Telegram. Each merchant creates their own bot with @BotFather and submits its token in the PG dashboard. The PG sends payment messages through that bot, and the app receives them through the branch phone's Telegram session (TDLib).

Merchant setup (one time, about 2 minutes)

  1. In Telegram, open @BotFather, send /newbot, and choose a name and username (for example KopiSenjaKasirBot).
  2. Copy the bot token and paste it into the Soundbox settings of the PG dashboard.
  3. The PG checks the token with getMe, registers its own webhook for that bot, and shows the bot username.
  4. The PG generates the per-outlet pairing QRs (section 1), which carry this bot's username.

How it runs

StepWhat happens
PairingThe app sends /start <pairing_token> to the merchant's bot. The PG receives it on that bot's webhook, links the chat to the outlet, and replies once with the device credential.
EventsFor each settled payment, the PG calls sendMessage with the merchant's token to every branch chat paired to that outlet.
Offline catch-upTelegram keeps the messages; the app reads them when it reconnects.
Merchant needsA Telegram account on each branch phone and one bot per merchant. One bot serves all of a merchant's outlets.

On redemption the PG returns, once, a device credential:

{ "type": "pairing.ok", "device_id": "dev_51a2", "outlet_id": "OUT-0012", "hmac_key": "base64..." }

Why one bot per merchant

  • Telegram rate limits apply per bot (about 1 message per second per chat, 30 per second overall), so load spreads across merchants instead of piling onto one shared PG bot.
  • The merchant owns and names the bot. Revoking the token in @BotFather cuts the channel at once.
  • The PG operates no public bot of its own.

What the PG must handle

  • Store bot tokens encrypted, as secrets, and never display them back in full.
  • Own the bot's update channel. Telegram allows one webhook (or getUpdates) per bot, so the merchant must not connect the same bot to another service. The dashboard should say so.
  • Detect revoked tokens: when sendMessage returns 401 Unauthorized, alert the merchant to submit a new token.

A leaked token lets someone send messages as the bot, but the app announces only events signed with the device hmac_key, so forged payments are rejected.

3. Dynamic QRIS (amount from POS)

The soundbox can capture the order amount from the POS by acting as the receipt printer the POS prints to. For dynamic QRIS, that amount goes up to the PG over the same Telegram bot, and the PG answers with a QRIS for exactly that amount. The branch phone's Telegram session can message the merchant's bot, and the PG already receives that bot's messages on its webhook.

Request: app → PG (message to the merchant's bot)

{
  "v": 1,
  "type": "qr.request",
  "request_id": "req_7Q2K9M",
  "device_id": "dev_51a2",
  "outlet_id": "OUT-0012",
  "amount": 25000,
  "currency": "IDR",
  "pos_ref": "INV-20261109-0042",
  "ts": 1762737012,
  "sig": "hex(HMAC-SHA256(hmac_key, canonical body without sig))"
}

Response: PG → app (bot reply in the same chat)

{
  "v": 1,
  "type": "qr.created",
  "request_id": "req_7Q2K9M",
  "outlet_id": "OUT-0012",
  "amount": 25000,
  "qris": "00020101021226...6304E1F2",
  "expires_at": "2026-11-10T08:45:00+07:00",
  "ts": 1762737013,
  "sig": "..."
}

If the PG cannot create the QR, it replies qr.failed with the same request_id and a short reason.

Rules

  • The PG accepts a qr.request only from a chat paired to that outlet, with a valid signature and ts within 5 minutes.
  • request_id is the idempotency key. A retried request returns the same QR, never a second one.
  • When the order is cancelled, the app sends qr.cancel with the request_id, and the PG voids the QR.
  • The payment.succeeded event for a dynamic QR carries the same request_id, so the soundbox marks that exact order as paid.
  • Suggested target: qr.created within 3 seconds. If nothing arrives within 8 seconds, the app falls back to the outlet's static QRIS with the amount embedded (the soundbox already does this conversion locally), so the cashier is never stuck. Payment still settles and is announced through the static flow.

4. Payment event

Sent once per settled QRIS payment for the paired outlet, within 3 seconds of settlement where possible.

{
  "v": 1,
  "type": "payment.succeeded",
  "event_id": "evt_01HZX8...",
  "device_id": "dev_51a2",
  "outlet_id": "OUT-0012",
  "amount": 25000,
  "currency": "IDR",
  "paid_at": "2026-11-09T21:35:12+07:00",
  "issuer": "GoPay",
  "rrn": "123456789012",
  "reference": "INV-20261109-0042",
  "request_id": "req_7Q2K9M",
  "ts": 1762699112,
  "sig": "hex(HMAC-SHA256(hmac_key, canonical body without sig))"
}
  • amount is an integer in rupiah. The soundbox speaks this value.
  • issuer and reference are optional and shown on screen only. request_id appears only for dynamic QRIS created through qr.request.
  • Every message text is #QRISSB followed by the JSON, so the app can ignore any other bot chatter.

Security and reliability

A soundbox is only useful if cashiers can trust it, so the event channel must be impossible to fake and must not double-announce.

Security

  • The pairing QR is shown only inside the PG's logged-in merchant area. The pairing_token is one-time and expires in about 10 minutes.
  • Every message is signed with HMAC-SHA256 using the per-device hmac_key. The app drops unsigned or wrongly signed messages and anything with ts more than 5 minutes off.
  • One device credential per paired phone. The merchant can see and revoke paired phones in the PG dashboard.
  • Events carry only what the counter needs: amount, time, issuer, reference. No customer name, phone number or account data.

Reliability

  • event_id is unique and stable across retries. The app keeps recent IDs and never announces the same one twice.
  • The PG retries delivery until the Telegram Bot API accepts the message, for at least 10 minutes.
  • Late events still display on the phone, but the soundbox does not speak events older than 2 minutes, to avoid confusing the cashier.
  • Suggested delivery target: 95% of events within 3 seconds of settlement.

Limits

The app does not confirm settlement and is not a source of truth. The PG dashboard remains the record for disputes.

Proposed rollout

  1. Agree on the v1 schema and the bot-token field in the PG dashboard.
  2. PG provides a sandbox: test outlet, pairing QR and simulated payments.
  3. QRIS Soundbox adds the PG adapter and runs end-to-end tests in sandbox.
  4. Pilot with 5–10 PG merchants, then general availability as an opt-in dashboard feature.

Open questions for the PG

  • Can your dashboard store a merchant's bot token securely and call the Telegram Bot API on their behalf?
  • Is your outlet or terminal ID available at settlement time for both static and dynamic QRIS?
  • Can you create a dynamic QRIS per request within about 3 seconds, and what expiry do you use?
  • Can the pairing QR live in your merchant app as well as the web dashboard?
  • Commercial model: free feature for merchants, co-marketing, or bundled soundbox sales?
